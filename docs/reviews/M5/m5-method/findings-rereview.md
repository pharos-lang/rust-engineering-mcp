# G8 Re-Review — M5 method corrections (`ai/m5-performance`)

**Commit reviewed: `875c8902e737b28cc432ebe5f886de0bd41b0503`.** During the review the branch advanced to `91a71aed8e81b54fa5a8ca40934c23cd83412831`; `git diff --name-status 875c890 HEAD` shows only two modifications (`docs/adr/ADR-077-m5-runtime-admission.md`, `scripts/test-m5-runtime.py`) plus added client-qualification evidence. No file bearing on any finding below differs between the two, and I read the working tree. One consequence is recorded at the end of "Verified".

## Verdict

**Pass.** The defect this re-review exists to test is fixed, and I verified the fix rather than the prose. The resampling unit really is the execution; the interval really does now carry between-execution variance; and the new corpus independently re-demonstrates the old defect and its repair — on the six admitted-image captures, the v1 resampling unit still produces a **false `regression` for `m5/slower_125`, whose source is byte-identical on both sides** (CI `+0.0528..+0.1363`, MDR `0.0390 ≤ 0.05`), while v2 on the same bytes returns `inconclusive` (CI `−0.0489..+0.3034`, MDR `0.3036`). Nothing frozen moved: resamples, seed, seed derivation, confidence level, threshold, sample floor, power and both z constants are byte-identical to the pre-fix tree; only the method identifier changed, correctly, to v2. `MAX_RESOLVABLE_FAMILY_SIZE = 25` is genuinely derived and the arithmetic checks out by hand. The seed remains grind-resistant under the new sampler. The six captures are hash-matched to their receipt, the guest hashed its own `/source` before every execution, and the six source hashes differ only in `src/lib.rs` — which closes the previous review's own stated limitation. The M5-01 "blocked" oracle does not launder red into green: it recomputes three predicates against the real `SOURCE_MAX_*` constants, fails the moment any stops being broken, and its receipt records `outcome: "blocked"`. I found no P0 and no P1: I could not construct a false published verdict from any committed byte, and in the delivered runtime the tool cannot emit a direction at all.

What I do report is six P2s. The most substantive is statistical: the cluster bootstrap is estimated from two or three clusters, so the published `confidence_level: 0.95` is not the coverage the interval delivers — 0.84–0.89 at the default `run_count = 3`, and 0.66–0.75 at `run_count = 2`, which is a published, caller-selectable input. That is an enormous improvement on v1 (0.29 at the same drift) and it is latent, not live, because the governor rule blocks every direction; but it is the residue of the same problem and it is undisclosed. The rest are publication defects: the output schema, `docs/tools.md` and ADR-076 all tell a caller the harness logs live in the `criterion_archive` artifact, and they do not live anywhere; the published limitation says the tool emits "no direction" when in fact `inconclusive` is the *only* verdict it can emit; and the disposition's account of its own corrections is wrong in at least ten places, including one it inherited verbatim from the finding it claims to have closed. None of these falsifies a verdict or a measurement, so none blocks — but the disposition should not be filed as the record of this milestone until it matches the tree.

## Findings

### P2-1 — The interval's published confidence level is not the coverage it delivers; the gap is worst at `run_count = 2`, which the tool accepts

`crates/domain/src/benchmark_compare.rs:792-809` (`cluster_draw`), `:820-854` (`bootstrap_ratio`), `:266` and `:270` (`confidence_level` / `adjusted_confidence_level`, both published), `:1137-1140` (the `>= 2` executions gate), `crates/mcp-server/tests/snapshots/benchmark-run-tool.json` (`run_count`: `minimum 1, maximum 3, default 3`).

**What is wrong.** The two-stage draw is the right construction, but the outer stage draws `k` clusters with replacement from the `k` the side ran, and `k` is 2 or 3. For a statistic that behaves like a mean over clusters, the nonparametric cluster bootstrap's variance has expectation `((k−1)/k)·σ²_between`, i.e. the SE is understated by `sqrt(k/(k−1))` — 1.41× at `k = 2`, 1.22× at `k = 3` — and the percentile endpoints use that distribution's own quantiles rather than a `t_{k−1}` correction. The method neither corrects for this nor discloses it; `ComparisonMethod` publishes `confidence_level: 0.95` and `adjusted_confidence_level` as if they were delivered.

**Concrete failing case.** I transcribed the module independently (SplitMix64, `index`, `median_sorted`, `quantile_sorted`, `cluster_draw`, `bootstrap_ratio`, `decide`) into Python and Rust and validated the transcription against the crate's own published numbers: comparing `m5/control` alone, baseline `criterion-run-2.tar` vs `criterion-candidate.tar`, my transcription returns interval `(−0.1494, −0.0751)` and MDR `0.0490`, matching ADR-073's correction note to the last digit. Under a Gaussian random-effects null (true effect exactly 0, 30 samples per execution, 2% within-execution noise, `τ` = run-to-run drift SD, family = 1, 1000 trials):

| τ | coverage of the true 0 effect, `k = 3` | coverage, `k = 2` | coverage, v1 (one pooled cluster) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0% | 0.985 | 0.981 | 0.965 |
| 1% | 0.889 | 0.842 | 0.648 |
| 2% | 0.839 | 0.751 | 0.467 |
| 5% | 0.843 | 0.705 | 0.286 |
| 10% | 0.863 | 0.657 | 0.184 |

and false directional verdicts under that same null, out of 1000:

| τ | `k = 3` | `k = 2` |
|---|---|---|
| 3% | 3 (0.3%) | 11 (1.1%) |
| 5% | 5 (0.5%) | **27 (2.7%)** |
| 10% | 3 (0.3%) | 14 (1.4%) |

At `k = 2` and τ = 5%, 27 of 1000 comparisons of *identical code* emit `regression` or `improvement`, with the believed SE understated 1.5–1.7× (measured `actualSD/believedSE`: 1.43 at τ = 2%, 1.51 at 5%, 1.65 at 10%). `run_count = 2` is not exotic: it is a published input with range 1..=3, it is validated end-to-end (`crates/application/src/benchmark.rs:271-274` requires `provenance.run_count == options.run_count()`), the `>= 2` gate at `:1137` admits it, and the native qualification names a selection `pooled-run-count-2`.

**Magnitude is model-dependent; direction is not.** The `sqrt(k/(k−1))` understatement is a property of the estimator, not of my noise model, and my measured ratios match it. The absolute false rates depend on the Gaussian drift model.

**Why it is not blocking.** Nothing here can reach a caller today: the governor rule (finding P2-3) refuses every direction, and on the real corpus the MDR is 0.19–0.30, five times the gate. The published limitation is overstated confidence, not a falsified verdict.

**Fix.** Cheapest correct option: raise the gate at `:1137` from `< 2` to `< 3`, so a direction requires the protocol's own default; that removes the whole `k = 2` row. Otherwise use a `t_{k−1}` quantile for the endpoints, or publish the delivered coverage beside `confidence_level` so the field stops claiming more than it is.

### P2-2 — The published schema, `docs/tools.md` and ADR-076 all say the harness logs live in the `criterion_archive` artifact; they are published nowhere

`crates/mcp-server/src/stdio/benchmark/schemas.rs:269-271`, `crates/mcp-server/tests/snapshots/benchmark-run-tool.json:531` (the same string, frozen into the public contract), `docs/tools.md:1267`, `docs/adr/ADR-076-m5-performance-contracts.md:62`, against `crates/mcp-server/src/stdio/quality_artifacts/performance.rs:285-311`.

The schema description reads: *"Harness logs are evidence, not a measurement: they stay in the criterion archive artifact. Only the fact that they were cut travels here."* `docs/tools.md:1267` repeats it (`criterion_archive`, *"que es donde quedan los logs"*), and ADR-076:62 says `harness_unrecognized` *"reporta ejecución, exit y logs"*.

**Concrete failing case.** The `criterion_archive` payload is a USTAR export of `CRITERION_HOME`. `tar -tf fixtures/benchmark-datasets/criterion-baseline-1.tar` lists 35 entries: `./`, `./m5/`, and per benchmark a `base/` and `new/` directory holding `benchmark.json`, `estimates.json`, `sample.json`, `tukey.json`. There is no log of any kind. Meanwhile `benchmark_members` (`performance.rs:285-311`) publishes exactly two members — the dataset and the tar — and `Data.artifacts` is schema-capped at 2; `observation.stdout` / `observation.stderr` are captured by the adapter (`crates/execution-adapter/src/performance_port.rs:474-480`) and then discarded. So a caller whose `rust.benchmark.run` returns `OBSERVED_FAILURE` gets `stdout_truncated` / `stderr_truncated` booleans, two artifacts, and is directed by the frozen schema to an archive that cannot contain the compiler or harness error text. For `harness_unrecognized` it is worse: `publish_benchmark` returns `Ok(Vec::new())` when there is no dataset, so no artifact at all is published, and ADR-076's "reporta … logs" is empty on the wire. The native oracle at `crates/execution-adapter/src/performance_native.rs:791-794` asserts the logs are non-empty, which is true of the adapter's in-memory observation and reinforces the wrong impression about the response.

**Fix.** Either publish the logs (a `ToolLog` member already exists as a kind), or correct the three published statements to say the logs are not retained.

### P2-3 — In this runtime the tool can emit exactly one verdict; the published limitation says only that it emits "no direction"

`crates/domain/src/benchmark_compare.rs:1153-1156`, `crates/execution-adapter/src/performance_port.rs:169-171`; published at `docs/tools.md:1359-1370`, `CHANGELOG.md:37-43`, `docs/validation/M5/matrix.md:120-131`, `docs/validation/M5/handoff.md:100-108`.

The unknown-hardware rule is **correct**, and I checked it adversarially: `agreement()` at `:886-893` separates the three cases exactly as ADR-073 §5's amendment describes, `descriptor()` at `:903-915` routes them, and `one_side_blind_and_both_sides_blind_are_different_answers` (`:2221-2281`) pins all three *with a positive control* — the same two datasets with the governor observed on both sides do receive an `Improvement`. The consequence the owner records is also correct: `hardware_profile` hardcodes `cpu_governor: None`, so `NeitherObserved` holds on every dataset this runtime can produce, and `unobservable_hardware` sets in.

**What is under-published.** The gate at `:1153-1156` returns `Inconclusive` **before the interval is read at all** (`:1162-1172`). So `no_material_change` is unreachable too. Every comparison of every benchmark in every report from this runtime is `Inconclusive`. All four publications say the tool emits no *direction* and that it still "mide y publica el efecto"; none says that three of the four verdicts the schema enumerates are unreachable.

**Concrete failing case.** Two datasets from this runtime whose measurements are indistinguishable — the honest answer being `no_material_change` — return `Inconclusive` / `unobservable_hardware`. A caller reading `docs/tools.md:1360` ("no emite ninguna dirección") would reasonably expect that case to be answerable, and the milestone's own self-compare oracle (`criterion_dataset.rs:1577-1584`) accepts `NoMaterialChange | Inconclusive`, which reads as if both were live.

**On severity of the rule itself.** It is the right response and it is the honest one, with one caveat worth recording: the rule cannot distinguish "unobservable because hidden" from "unobservable because absent". On a host with no cpufreq driver — where the governor is unreadable *precisely because the clock is fixed* — a direction would in fact be sound and is refused anyway. The product offers no declaration channel out of it (nothing analogous to `--allow-profiling`), so the refusal is permanent on every host, not only this one. That is a design consequence, not an error; it should be stated as such rather than framed as a property of "el runtime M5 tal como se entrega".

**Fix.** One sentence in each of the four places: in this runtime `inconclusive` is the only verdict emitted, `no_material_change` included.

## Verified

Checked adversarially and found sound.

- **The resampling unit really did become the execution, and the interval really carries the between-execution term.** `SideSamples::of` (`:748-759`) groups by `run_index` through a `BTreeMap`, so cluster order is a function of the data; `cluster_draw` (`:792-809`) draws `k` clusters with replacement and then that cluster's own sample count with replacement; `bootstrap_ratio` (`:820-854`) recomputes both medians per resample. My independent transcription reproduces the crate's published numbers exactly, including the single-cluster control ADR-073 quotes: `(−0.1494, −0.0751)`, MDR `0.0490`.
- **The correction is demonstrated on the newest bytes, not only asserted.** Recomputing the *v1* resampling unit over the six admitted-image captures at the report's real family size of 3: `m5/slower_125`, whose source is identical on both sides, gives CI `(+0.0528, +0.1363)` and MDR `0.0390` — a **false `regression`**. v2 gives `(−0.0489, +0.3034)`, MDR `0.3036`, `inconclusive`. `m5/reference` (the one changed benchmark) is `+0.2433` under both, with the interval widening from `(+0.2196, +0.2623)` to `(+0.0795, +0.4109)`. Coverage of a true zero effect rises from 0.29 (v1, τ = 5%) to 0.84 (v2, `k = 3`).
- **Nothing frozen moved.** Diffing the constants block against `4fc2f0c~1`: `BOOTSTRAP_RESAMPLES = 10_000`, `BOOTSTRAP_SEED = 0x0005_EEDB_0075_7241`, `CONFIDENCE_LEVEL = 0.95`, `MATERIAL_THRESHOLD_RATIO = 0.05`, `MIN_SAMPLES_FOR_INFERENCE = 10`, `DETECTION_POWER = 0.80`, `Z_TWO_SIDED_95`, `Z_POWER_80` — all identical. Two constants added, one identifier changed to v2. The identifier change is correct: v1 and v2 intervals genuinely are not comparable.
- **`MAX_RESOLVABLE_FAMILY_SIZE = 25` is derived, and the arithmetic is right.** `10 000 × (0.05/25)/2 = 10.000 ≥ 10`; at 26, `9.615 < 10`. At the limit `quantile_sorted` positions the endpoint at `9999 × 0.001 = 9.999`, i.e. an interpolation between the tenth and eleventh draws, exactly as documented. `the_resolvable_family_size_is_derived_from_the_resample_budget` (`:2742-2765`) recomputes the largest `n` from `BOOTSTRAP_RESAMPLES`, `CONFIDENCE_LEVEL` and `MIN_TAIL_RESAMPLES` and asserts equality, so moving any of the three fails the test. `a_family_past_the_resolution_limit_claims_no_interval` (`:2771-2827`) additionally proves the boundary is live: 25 benchmarks yield `Regression` on data that 26 refuses, and the refused report still publishes the observed ratio, sample counts and outliers with `(0.0, 0.0)` for the interval.
- **Seed derivation is unchanged and still grind-resistant under the new sampler.** `for_benchmark` (`:563-571`) is the same FNV-1a fold into the fixed root; the stream is a function of the benchmark key only. I ran 200 distinct benchmark names over the real `m5/reference` corpus (endpoints moved ≤ 0.0098, MDR ≤ 0.0081, zero verdict changes) and over 15 synthetic configurations placed at the decision boundary, including cases whose lower endpoint sat at 0.0535–0.0540 against the 0.05 threshold: endpoints moved ≤ 0.0018, MDR ≤ 0.0012, and **not one of the 3000 runs flipped a verdict**. `a_benchmark_result_does_not_depend_on_its_position_in_the_report` (`:2830`) and `comparing_the_same_two_datasets_twice_is_byte_identical` (`:2953`) still hold.
- **The four refusals are ordered so each precedes the reading it would invalidate**, and each is placed where its early return is defensible: single-execution before the interval (`:1137`) because a one-cluster resample reproduces the v1-narrow interval — which the ADR proves rather than asserts; family before dispersion (`:1141`, `:1145`) because skipping the bootstrap leaves an empty distribution, not a degenerate measurement; unobservable hardware (`:1153`) after the sample-quality gates because it is about the ambient condition and not the samples. I walked every branch looking for a case where a refusal fires but the direction would have been sound. Two exist and both are conservative and documented: `FamilyBeyondResolution` refuses a 26-benchmark target however large the effect (and the caller's only lever is `bench_target`, chosen before measuring), and `UnobservableHardware` refuses on a host with no cpufreq at all. Neither produces a wrong claim.
- **`DegenerateDispersion` closes the hole it was built for.** `outcome.degenerate` is read off the draw distribution rather than off `standard_error > 0.0` (`:769-781`, `:841-845`), which is the right call: 10 000 identical values summed leave a rounding residue, and the previous review's exact case (30 constant baseline samples, 30 constant candidate samples 6% apart) produces identical ratios and is caught, even though `fewer_than_two_distinct_values` alone would not catch it.
- **The three-way hardware rule is pinned with a positive control**, and `every_unobservable_descriptor_withholds_the_direction` (`:2286-2316`) extends it to `cpu_model`, `cpu_cores`, `os_kernel` and `Virtualization::Unknown`. `configuration_fingerprint` is now consulted (`:966-968`), closing the previous P2-2; I checked it is a digest over the normalized gateway configuration (`rust_gateway.rs:1014`, volatile names replaced by placeholders), so it is stable across runs and does not accidentally refuse every comparison.
- **The compare output carries both provenances on both paths** (`:466-467`, `:479-480`), on refusal *and* on success, with `provenance_projection_carries_every_consulted_field` (`:2345`) holding the projection and the check's field set together. The previous P2-3 is genuinely closed.
- **The six captures support what is claimed of them.** All six committed tars match the receipt's `sha256` and `bytes` exactly. Every median in the receipt matches my independent recomputation from the raw `times`/`iters` to the published decimal (e.g. baseline-1 `m5/reference` 3140.47, candidate-3 `m5/control` 3077.58). The receipt's `median_of_medians` and `spread` values reproduce. Most importantly, the guest hashed its own read-only `/source` before every execution and the receipt publishes all four hashes per capture: `Cargo.lock`, `Cargo.toml` and `benches/perf.rs` are identical across all six, and `src/lib.rs` differs only between sides. That closes the previous review's own stated limitation — that the candidate's source could not be verified from the tar — and turns "only `work_unit` changed" from a README claim into a receipted fact.
- **The `admitted_image_datasets` oracle asserts something discriminating.** `mdr > 0.05` is the load-bearing assertion and it is a real guard: under a v1 resampling unit the same six captures give MDR `0.0298` for `m5/reference` and the assertion fails. `three_executions_a_side_pool_into_one_dataset_that_knows_its_executions` fails if pooling ever loses `run_index`. I note the sibling, `a_benchmark_whose_source_never_changed_is_never_given_a_direction`, is **vacuous in this runtime** — the governor gate forces `Inconclusive` for every benchmark before the interval is read, so it would pass under any bootstrap whatsoever — but the domain-level oracle `drift_between_executions_is_not_read_as_a_difference_between_datasets` (`:2011-2068`) carries that burden instead, with 6% between-execution drift, 0.5% within-execution jitter, and an explicit `minimum_detectable_ratio > MATERIAL_THRESHOLD_RATIO`.
- **The M5-01 "blocked" oracle pins the blocker and fails when it lifts.** `m5_benchmark_run_positive_is_blocked_by_the_offline_data_bound` (`performance_native.rs:896-959`) measures the materialized vendor tree and asserts all three bounds are broken *separately* against the real domain constants `SOURCE_MAX_ENTRIES`, `SOURCE_MAX_TOTAL_BYTES`, `SOURCE_MAX_FILE_BYTES` (`crates/domain/src/source.rs:4-8`); if any one stops being broken — whether because the closure shrank or because someone raised a bound — it returns an error whose message demands the positive be implemented and qualified. The receipt records `outcome: "blocked"` for both selections with the four named oversized files, and the matrix, handoff and CHANGELOG all say **Blocked**. Nothing is laundered. Two narrow observations: the oracle recomputes the predicates rather than constructing a `SourceBundle` and observing the product's own refusal (that enforcement is covered by `source.rs:169-190`), and it is `#[ignore]`d — but `scripts/test-m5-runtime.py:200-209` enumerates every ignored test in the file and runs them all, so it is inside the gate.

## Limitations of this review

- I ran no Docker and no native cut. Every runtime claim rests on receipts, which I cross-checked against each other, against the source, and against the committed bytes.
- I ran `cargo test --workspace --offline --locked` but not clippy, `scripts/gate.py`, or any ignored native test.
- P2-1's coverage and false-rate magnitudes come from a Gaussian random-effects drift model. The direction and the `sqrt(k/(k−1))` mechanism are properties of the estimator and hold regardless; the specific percentages do not. Everything else — the v1-vs-v2 comparison on the six captures, the reproduction of ADR-073's published numbers, the seed-grinding sweeps, the family-size arithmetic, the capture digests — is recomputation on committed bytes and is model-free.
- I did not re-audit profiling containment, SVG sanitisation, the bloat vertical beyond its published limitation, or the M2/M4 code these tools sit on.
